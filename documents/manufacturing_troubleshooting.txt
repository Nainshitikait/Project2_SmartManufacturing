MANUFACTURING DEFECT TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE

Document Type: Manufacturing Quality and Troubleshooting Guide
Purpose: Provide basic guidance for identifying and handling manufacturing defects.


1. NO DEFECT

Description:
The sensor readings are within the expected operating range and no significant defect pattern is detected.

Recommended Action:
Continue normal production and perform routine quality monitoring.


2. MINOR DEFECT

Description:
A minor defect indicates a small deviation from normal manufacturing conditions. The component may require inspection before continuing production.

Common Indicators:
- Slight increase in vibration
- Small dimensional deviation
- Small increase in surface roughness
- Minor temperature deviation

Recommended Action:
- Inspect the affected component.
- Check machine vibration.
- Verify dimensional measurements.
- Monitor the next production cycles.
- Perform routine maintenance if the abnormal condition continues.


3. MODERATE DEFECT

Description:
A moderate defect indicates a noticeable deviation from normal manufacturing conditions and requires engineering attention.

Common Indicators:
- Increased vibration
- Noticeable dimensional deviation
- Increased surface roughness
- Significant temperature deviation
- Increased material stress

Recommended Action:
- Inspect the component immediately.
- Check machine alignment and operating conditions.
- Verify dimensional measurements.
- Review vibration and temperature readings.
- Consider temporarily stopping the affected production process.
- Perform maintenance before restarting if the abnormal condition persists.


4. CRITICAL DEFECT

Description:
A critical defect indicates a severe abnormal condition that may affect component quality or manufacturing safety.

Common Indicators:
- Very high vibration
- Large dimensional deviation
- High temperature deviation
- High material stress
- Severe surface roughness

Recommended Action:
- Stop the affected production process.
- Isolate the affected component or batch.
- Perform detailed engineering inspection.
- Check machine condition and alignment.
- Investigate the root cause.
- Perform corrective maintenance before restarting production.
- Verify component quality after corrective action.


5. VIBRATION TROUBLESHOOTING

High vibration may indicate machine imbalance, misalignment, loose components, bearing problems, or abnormal operating conditions.

Recommended Action:
- Inspect machine alignment.
- Check mechanical connections.
- Inspect bearings and rotating components.
- Verify operating conditions.
- Monitor vibration after corrective maintenance.


6. TEMPERATURE TROUBLESHOOTING

Abnormal temperature deviation may indicate excessive friction, cooling problems, abnormal machine operation, or process instability.

Recommended Action:
- Check the cooling system.
- Inspect machine operating conditions.
- Check for excessive friction.
- Monitor temperature during subsequent production cycles.


7. DIMENSIONAL DEVIATION

Dimensional deviation indicates that a manufactured component differs from the expected dimensional specification.

Recommended Action:
- Recheck the measurement.
- Inspect machine calibration.
- Check tooling condition.
- Verify machine alignment.
- Perform additional quality inspection.


8. SURFACE ROUGHNESS

Increased surface roughness may indicate tooling problems, machine vibration, incorrect machining conditions, or tool wear.

Recommended Action:
- Inspect the cutting tool or machining tool.
- Check machine vibration.
- Verify machining parameters.
- Inspect the component surface again after corrective action.


9. MATERIAL STRESS

High material stress may indicate excessive loading, manufacturing process instability, or material-related problems.

Recommended Action:
- Review manufacturing process conditions.
- Inspect the affected component.
- Check material specifications.
- Perform engineering analysis if the abnormal stress continues.
